🚨Algorithm 1 — Status Epilepticus

0 min

Immediate Stabilization

  • Airway / breathing / circulation
  • Oxygen
  • Cardiorespiratory monitoring
  • IV/IO access
  • Point-of-care glucose
  • Electrolytes + relevant laboratory evaluation
  • Search for reversible cause
First-Line — Benzodiazepine

Give Benzodiazepine

If IV/IO access is not achieved within 3 minutes — give midazolam IM (10 mg or 0.2 mg/kg), buccal (10 mg), or intranasal (10 mg — 5 mg in each nostril if using the nasal-spray formulation).

ICU-Level Management

DrugDose
MidazolamLoad 0.2 mg/kg IV; start 0.1 mg/kg/hr; titrate to seizure freedom; max 3 mg/kg/hr
PropofolLoad 1–2 mg/kg over 5 min, may repeat 0.5–2 mg/kg; start infusion 20 mcg/kg/min; titrate; max 200 mcg/kg/min (max total 5 mg/kg/hr)
PentobarbitalLoad 5 mg/kg IV over 10 min (max rate 50 mg/min), additional 5 mg/kg if seizures continue; start 1 mg/kg/hr; titrate; max 5 mg/kg/hr
Continuous EEG and ICU-level monitoring are indicated for refractory status epilepticus, and whenever a patient fails to return to baseline after apparent seizure cessation.

Epilepsy is defined as ≥2 unprovoked seizures more than 24 hours apart, OR one unprovoked seizure with a sufficiently high recurrence risk, OR an epilepsy syndrome.
01 Seizure Semiology — What Did It Look Like?▸

Focal

  • Awareness preserved → Focal aware
  • Awareness impaired → Focal impaired awareness
  • Then bilateral tonic-clonic → Focal to bilateral tonic-clonic

Generalized

  • Generalized tonic-clonic
  • Absence
  • Myoclonic
  • Clonic
  • Tonic
  • Atonic

03 Postictal = Information▸

Postictal findings to document:

  • Confusion / somnolence
  • Todd paresis
  • Aphasia
  • Hemianopia
  • Numbness
Persistent failure to return to baseline → think ongoing nonconvulsive seizure → EEG.
04 First-Seizure Workup▸

Labs — Always Think

  • Glucose
  • Na
  • Ca
  • Mg
  • CBC
  • Renal function
  • LFTs
  • Lactate
  • Urinalysis

Conditional: toxicology (if exposure suspected), pregnancy test (when relevant).

CT vs MRI

CT firstEmergency/concerning presentation — persistent altered mental status, focal deficit, fever, persistent headache, focal-onset seizure, acute trauma, malignancy, immunocompromise, anticoagulation/bleeding risk
MRI preferredStable patient / outpatient setting
ORDER — COPY/PASTEMRI BRAIN — EPILEPSY PROTOCOL

3D T1 · T2/STIR · FLAIR · susceptibility-sensitive sequence · thin contiguous slices · dedicated hippocampal/coronal imaging

EEG Decision

Not back to baselineUrgent EEG / cEEG — especially persistent altered mental status, concern for NCSE, refractory SE, unexplained prolonged deficit
Back to baselineOutpatient EEG
A normal EEG does not exclude epilepsy.

Do I Need an LP?

  • Fever / meningismus / encephalitic picture → yes
  • Concern for meningeal malignancy / chronic meningitis → yes
  • SAH concern with nondiagnostic imaging → yes, follow SAH pathway
  • Otherwise → usually not routine
LP can be misleading after prolonged convulsive seizures. Exclude a space-occupying lesion first when LP is otherwise indicated.
05 Seizure Mimics — Source-Based Differential▸

Seizure diagnosis is clinical and requires differentiating it from several mimics:

  • Syncope
  • Convulsive syncope
  • TIA
  • Migraine
  • Panic/anxiety
  • Functional/dissociative seizure
  • Transient global amnesia
  • Narcolepsy/cataplexy
  • Paroxysmal movement disorder
  • Sleep disorders
Lateral tongue bite is a highly specific clue for epileptic seizure — but its absence does NOT rule out seizure. Specificity is high; sensitivity is relatively low. Urinary incontinence has much lower diagnostic utility.
A structured seizure-vs-syncope-vs-functional-seizure-vs-migraine comparison table (onset, motor activity, duration, recovery, tongue bite, incontinence, triggers, post-event state) is planned as a separately sourced evidence module — not fabricated here from this page's source material.
06 Disposition — Admit or Discharge?▸

Admit / Observe If

  • Prolonged postictal state
  • Incomplete recovery
  • Serious seizure-related injury
  • Status epilepticus
  • Serious neurologic/systemic illness
  • Inability to complete outpatient workup reliably

Possible Discharge If

  • Returned to baseline
  • Initial evaluation reassuring
  • No major acute illness/injury
  • Reliable follow-up
07 Discharge & Safety — Don't Forget Before They Leave▸

Ask about and counsel on:

  • Driving restrictions
  • Swimming
  • Bathing
  • Heights
  • Heavy machinery
  • Sleep deprivation
  • Alcohol
  • Medications/substances
Driving restrictions vary by jurisdiction — check current state-specific driving restrictions rather than relying on a fixed interval.
